Question 1: Explain what linked genes are and why they cannot independently assort

Linked genes are located close together on the same chromosome. In meiosis, whole chromosomes separate into gametes, so genes on the same chromosome are carried into the same gamete together. They cannot assort independently of each other, so the parental combinations of alleles appear in most of the offspring instead of the 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 ratio of independent genes.

Question 2: Explain how linked alleles may be separated by crossing over

In prophase I, homologous chromosomes pair up and non-sister chromatids swap sections of DNA. If the crossover falls between the two genes, the alleles on a chromatid are exchanged, making recombinant chromatids (B v and b V). Gametes with these chromatids give recombinant offspring. The further apart the genes are, the more often crossing over separates them, and genes very far apart give results like independent assortment (50% recombinants).

Test cross detective

What is a test cross? A cross between an individual that shows the dominant phenotype but has an unknown genotype and a homozygous recessive individual. Because the recessive parent only passes on recessive alleles, the phenotypes of the offspring reveal the unknown parent's genotype.

How it is carried out:

  1. Choose the individual with the dominant phenotype. It could be homozygous dominant or heterozygous.
  2. Cross it with a homozygous recessive individual.
  3. Produce a large number of offspring.
  4. Count the offspring of each phenotype.
  5. All dominant offspring means the parent is homozygous dominant. About half recessive means it is heterozygous.

The Punnett squares behind it

Pea plants: purple (P) is dominant to white (p). The unknown purple plant is crossed with a white plant (pp).

All offspring are Pp, so all are purple.

If the unknown plant is Pp

Half the offspring are Pp (purple) and half are pp (white).
